# Clinical content policy

This is the authoritative policy for clinical facts in the standalone ECG Lab. It defines a workflow boundary; it does not supply medical guidance. The current project contains no published clinical lesson, question, case, or patient recording.

## Medical accuracy and India-first context

Future clinical content must have an identified educational purpose, traceable support for its specific claims, and review appropriate to its risk. India is the primary context for Indian medical learners. Where applicable, content authors should identify relevant Indian medical education or clinical sources (including NMC/CBME and Indian professional guidance) and state their scope and version. Guideline references require an issuing organization and explicit jurisdiction. International guidance may be used as complementary material only when clearly labelled; it must not silently replace Indian context.

Do not invent guidelines, competency codes, drug brands, doses, clinical recommendations, prior questions, evidence, reviewers, qualifications, or statistics. If authoritative evidence or an appropriate Indian source is unavailable, say so and do not present an unsupported recommendation as fact. Candidate source metadata is attached to three draft lessons, but it is marked unverified and is not treated as substantiation or approval.

## Synthetic signal boundary

Synthetic signals and simplified morphology templates are educational illustrations. They are not patient recordings, diagnostic tools, or validated patient physiology. Mathematically consistent lead derivation, calibration, and schema validation do not establish clinical validity. Synthetic provenance and limitations must remain visible in learner-facing contexts.

## Publication controls

No clinical record may be presented as published or medically reviewed solely because it passes validation. Publication requires the independent checks in `CONTENT_REVIEW_WORKFLOW.md`: technical/schema validation; source and licensing/authorization verification; educational review; qualified medical-content review for clinical material; and separate publication approval. Clinical cases require a supported answer/explanation, verified references linked to relevant objectives or claims, explicit known limitations, and valid calibration metadata for linked ECG content.

Reviewer identity, qualifications, review dates, publication details, and licences must reflect actual records. Missing data remains missing and blocks any badge or publication gate that depends on it. The schema records workflow evidence; it cannot prove clinical correctness, licensure, identity, legal sufficiency, or source truth.

See `CONTENT_SCHEMA.md` for runtime rules and `CONTENT_PROVENANCE.md` for data rights and source management.
